THE IDEA, NOT THE TAKE

Same speaker, a different thought — rebuilt from three places

Not a cut — an assembly

~27 seconds, spliced from three moments 74 and 20 seconds apart

The promise at the top of this page — "I go through all of it and pull out the moments that actually hold attention" — is what this clip is built to prove, not just say. Three spoken moments, none of them adjacent, are spliced into one continuous idea: 74 seconds of talk sits between the first two pieces, 20 seconds between the last two, and none of that is in the clip. The cut points are chosen on word boundaries from a transcript, not by listening for a pause.

The illustration on "less than one percent" isn't a stock overlay dropped on top — it's colour-matched to the palette of the surrounding frames, grained to the noise level measured off the source footage, and given a slow 1.5% drift so it doesn't read as a pasted-in still. The music holds level, then cuts to near-silence just before that line and comes back right after — the pause does the work, not a volume swell.

HOW IT'S BUILT

The same clip, one layer at a time

One more piece of the same conversation — a different moment, taken because it's a single unbroken take. That's what makes it possible to pull the edit apart below and show exactly what each layer adds, one at a time, instead of just claiming it.

Same source clip in every step below — Elon Musk, on the Cheeky Pint x Dwarkesh Podcast, mid-thought ("I'm not sure AI is the main risk..."), not a warm-up line. Each step keeps everything from the one before it and adds exactly one thing — press play on any of them, or go left to right and the whole strip runs about two minutes twenty seconds. The line under each clip is what it adds; the line under that is what it's actually doing for the person watching.

Raw source

What I started from. Horizontal, no captions, no crop, nothing added.

Everything below is measured against this frame — nothing here is working for attention yet.

1

+ Captions

Word-by-word captions burned in, grouped by clause and punctuation. Still horizontal — nothing else changed yet.

Most people watch with sound off — the caption is often the only thing that lands.

2

+ Reframe

Cropped to vertical (9:16). The crop point is fixed here — tracking comes next.

Craft, not the point of it — on a phone, horizontal means a rotate or black bars, an easy reason to swipe past.

3

+ Speaker tracking

The crop locks onto his face instead of the geometric center of frame 2.

Still craft — a fixed crop clips him when he moves, and that reads as a mistake, not a style.

4

+ Cuts

This is the one that matters most: deciding which words actually make the cut — the sentence that carries the idea, not just whichever take came out smoothest to say.

Everything else on this page is craft on top of this decision — reframing, tracking, music and type mean nothing if the words underneath aren't the ones worth keeping.

5

+ Shot changes

Five crop-scale jumps built by hand on this one unbroken shot — medium, close, extreme close, back to medium, close again — timed to where the thought turns, not to a timer.

Craft on top of step 4's cut — a single unmoving frame reads as "phone on a stand", deliberate scale changes read as a real edit, even though the camera never cut.

6

+ Music

A background track comes in under the voice, mixed to stay under it.

Craft again — silence under a talking head can feel like the clip stalled, the bed track keeps the energy up without touching a word of what's said.

7

+ Effects

The clip's key phrase lands as large type, timed to the moment he says it — not an icon.

The last layer of craft — type big enough to read at a glance gives the eye a beat to land on exactly where the sentence peaks.

Step 7, said plainly: this is what "effects" means in my pipeline today — the key phrase set in large type at the exact second it's spoken, not a library of icons, animated transitions, or generative visuals. I'd rather show what it actually does, repeated, than promise more than that.

WHY THIS GETS MEASURED

Measured, not eyeballed

This isn't about being careful for its own sake — a caption that drifts or audio that clips is a clip that comes back for a redo, and a redo is days you don't get back. So the same two checks run on every edit before it ships, not eyeballed, not a one-off for this page. Ask any editor for their own numbers on these two things — most won't have an answer, because most don't measure.

01

Caption position

Measured with
style_probe.py — samples the caption's bounding box across 40 frames of the rendered clip and logs its Y-position in pixels.
Measured on
Every clip I ship, and the same check run on a comparable clip from another editor's public portfolio.
What it means
A tight range means the caption holds one safe position instead of jumping frame to frame. Mine: 210px range. The comparison clip: 494–1644px.
02

Audio loudness

Measured with
ffmpeg -af loudnorm=print_format=summary — reports integrated loudness (LUFS) and true peak (dBTP) for the finished mix.
Measured on
Every clip I ship, and the same comparison clip.
What it means
Broadcast norm caps true peak at −1 dBTP. Mine: −14 LUFS, no clipping. The comparison clip clipped its peak.
